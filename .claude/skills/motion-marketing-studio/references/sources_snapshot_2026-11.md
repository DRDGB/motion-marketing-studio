## PART B — Compliance for regulated (pharma / health) clients

### B1. Medicines advertising

**B1.1 United States (FDA / OPDP)**
- FACT-S: Under 21 CFR 202.1, Rx drug ads must present a "fair balance" of benefit and risk information. Broadcast ads need a **major statement** of major risks, plus either "adequate provision" or a brief summary. The FDA final rule of Nov 2023 (88 FR 80958) sets five standards for the major statement to be "clear, conspicuous and neutral" (CCN): effective 20 May 2024, compliance from 20 Nov 2024. The FDA CDER SBIA deck https://www.fda.gov/media/184312/download is on fda.gov; other details come via REPORTED summaries (Foley Hoag, July 2026: https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/publications/alerts-and-updates/2026/july/fda-proposes-to-eliminate-adequate-provision-for-broadcast-prescription-drug-advertising/).
- REPORTED: On **9 Sep 2025** a presidential memorandum directed HHS/FDA to tighten DTC enforcement. FDA sent "thousands" of letters, then released 60+ warning letters (16 Sep 2025) and more untitled letters (25 Sep 2025). Sources: Latham & Watkins https://www.lw.com/en/insights/fda-begins-crackdown-on-direct-to-consumer-pharmaceutical-advertising ; Foley Hoag Sept 2025 https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/publications/alerts-and-updates/2025/september/hhs-and-fda-attempt-to-crackdown-on-deceptive-dtc-rx-drug-advertising-following-presidential-memo/ ; Healio 2025-09-10.
- REPORTED: The 2026 Unified Agenda lists RIN 0910-AJ14, "Transparency in DTC Advertising", which would remove "adequate provision". An NPRM is expected in **Dec 2026**, so it is not yet final (Foley Hoag, July 2026). OPDP's 2026 letters judge the **net impression**, including risk information obscured by visuals, music or pacing (Arnall Golden Gregory https://www.agg.com/news-insights/publications/gimme-shelter-opdps-2026-letters-show-theres-no-shelter-from-fda-scrutiny-targeting-dtc-ads-that-obscure-risk/ ; Life Science Leader). One blog claims a 2026 final rule; this is UNVERIFIED and conflicts with the sources above.
- **Motion-design implication (INTERPRETATION):** fast cuts, upbeat music, or animation running over the risk statement are exactly the pattern OPDP now cites. Risk audio and supers must be legible, at the same pace, with no distracting visuals.

**B1.2 European Union (Directive 2001/83/EC, Title VIII "Advertising")**
- FACT-S: Art. 88 requires Member States to **prohibit advertising to the general public of prescription-only medicines** (and of medicines containing psychotropics or narcotics). Art. 88(6) bans direct distribution of medicines to the public for promotion. Art. 90 lists material banned from public ads; one item is third-party statements on recovery that are improper, alarming or misleading (CJEU C-374/05 *Gintec*). CJEU C-530/20 (2022) holds the Art. 90 list is **non-exhaustive**, so states may add bans. Sources: legislation.gov.uk copy of the Directive https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2001/83/article/90 ; Gintec https://www.ippt.eu/sites/default/files/2007/IPPT20071108_ECJ_Gintec.pdf ; PLMJ note (Jan 2023). Check consolidated text on EUR-Lex before quoting article letters.

**B1.3 United Kingdom**
- FACT-S: Human Medicines Regulations 2012 **reg. 284** bans ads to the public likely to lead to requests for a POM. MHRA Blue Guide App. 6: the POM ban "applies to social networks ... in the same way as for traditional media", and any mention of POMs on a home page is likely to count as advertising. Source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/824545/Appendix_6_-_Blue_Guide.pdf ; https://www.gov.uk/advertise-your-medicines. CAP Code rule 12.12 mirrors this. MHRA and the ASA run joint enforcement on POM weight-loss promotion on social media (ASA Enforcement Notice, Sept 2025 https://www.asa.org.uk/static/ce4c6aa1-4cb0-4fbb-8d53564b9174e1b7/Enforcement-Notice-prescription-only-medicines-used-for-weight-management-September-2025.pdf). MHRA investigation reports continue through July 2026 (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/advertising-investigations-july-2026/websites-offering-medicinal-treatment-services-july-2026).
- FACT-S: **ABPI Code 2024** (industry self-regulation via the PMCPA) took effect 1 Oct 2024 and became mandatory 1 Jan 2025. Clause 26 covers the public and media. PMCPA social-media guidance was updated Oct 2024 and revised again (REPORTED, Morgan Lewis, Feb 2026). It applies when there is a UK nexus. https://pmcpa.org.uk/media/r0anf5ya/2024-abpi-code.pdf

**B1.4 Côte d'Ivoire / UEMOA (most relevant to the first client)**
- FACT-S: **Décret n° 2016-717 du 14 septembre 2016** regulates advertising of **medicines, other health products and pharmaceutical establishments**. It was published in JORCI on 15 Dec 2016 and repeals Décret 98-473. Advertising must not undermine public health; information must be "exact, à jour, vérifiable et suffisamment complète", and advertising must favour rational use. Sources: ECOLEX https://www.ecolex.org/details/legislation/decret-n-2016-717-du-14-septembre-2016-portant-reglementation-de-la-publicite-des-medicaments-des-autres-produits-de-sante-et-des-etablissements-pharmaceutiques-lex-faoc224094/ ; full text PDF https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ivc224094.pdf (blocked here) ; consolidated version https://civlii.laws.africa/en/akn/ci/act/decree/2016/717/fra@2022-02-21/source (blocked).
- REPORTED: The decree requires an **Autorisation de publicité issued by the AIRP**. An advertising commission sits within the AIRP with several ministries taking part. Advertising of health products that is not authorised is prohibited. Sources: LEEM Afrique https://www.leemafrique.org/fr/reglementation-pharmaceutique-en-cote-d-ivoire.asp (n.d.); Afrik Soir https://afriksoir.net/cote-divoire-aucun-medicament-ne-doit-etre-vendu-sur-les-reseaux-sociaux-dg-airp/ (n.d.).
- REPORTED: At its **16 Sep 2026** restitution, the AIRP said that **any advertising with health claims must be approved by the Commission nationale d'autorisation de la publicité, and advertising broadcast without that approval is illegal and exposes its authors to sanctions**. The AIRP DG also warned against false health claims on social media, including AI-made ones. Sources: AIP https://www.aip.ci/cote-divoire-aip-lairp-appelle-les-acteurs-des-produits-de-sante-au-respect-des-exigences-reglementaires/ (Sept 2026); AllAfrica https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202609170316.html (2026-09-17); Fraternité Matin https://www.fratmat.info/article/2644557/... ; Koaci 2026-09-17.
- REPORTED: The AIRP head stated in **July 2026**: "Aucun médicament ne doit être vendu sur les réseaux sociaux. En Côte d'Ivoire, la vente en ligne des médicaments n'est pas encore autorisée." Source: Koaci 2026-07-03 https://www.koaci.com/article/2026/07/03/cote-divoire/sante/cote-divoire-11-849-produits-cosmetiques-recenses-lairp-declare-la-guerre-aux-faux-medicaments-et-aux-produits-eclaircissants_198240.html
- FACT-S: The AIRP was created by **Loi n° 2017-541 du 3 août 2017** (pharmaceutical sector regulation); it is organised by a 2018 decree. GUCE: importing advertising material requires the **advertising-authorisation notification plus a valid AMM** (marketing authorisation) for the product. https://www.gucecotedivoire.ci/pwic/en/ivorian-pharmaceutical-regulatory-authority-airp/
- REPORTED: The decree's sanctions (penal, administrative, civil) are mainly in **arts. 26–28** (student thesis, memoireonline, 2022). Under general consumer law (Loi 91-1000 of 27 Dec 1991 on misleading advertising, per thesis; Loi 2016-412 on consumer protection), the advertiser must substantiate its claims, and agencies and media are expected to verify them. A **Loi du 16 juin 2020 portant régime juridique de la communication publicitaire** refers medicine advertising to a specific decree (https://www.droitci.info/files/915.06.20-Loi-du-16-juin-2020-portant-regime-juridique-de-la-communication-publicitaire.pdf ; not read).
- REPORTED: Traditional practitioners (tradipraticiens) have been barred from "tout acte de publicité" since about Jan 2016 (government communiqué; Connectionivoirienne 2016-02-17). HACA reiterated this for radio in 2022. Relevant if the client sells phytomedicines.
- **UEMOA:** FACT-S/REPORTED: **Décision n° 10/2010/CM/UEMOA** adopts guidelines on the control of medicine **information and advertising aimed at health professionals** in UEMOA states. **No UEMOA text on public advertising was found (UNVERIFIED).**
- **NOT FOUND / UNVERIFIED:** the decree's exact article that bans or allows public advertising of Rx-only medicines, the visa validity period, mandatory mentions, the filing procedure and timelines, and the specific rules for officine (retail pharmacy) advertising. The Ivorian pharmacists' code of ethics (Décret 81-039; a 2015 law referenced) was not checked. The skill must not state any of these as fact until the decree text is read.
- **Pharmacy vs manufacturer (INTERPRETATION):** the decree covers "établissements pharmaceutiques" as well as products. Treat both a manufacturer's product ad and a pharmacy's own promotional content as needing AIRP review. Treat Rx-only medicines as not advertisable to the public, following the international norm (EU/UK). Seek written confirmation from the AIRP or a local regulatory advisor.

### B2. OTC products, supplements, testimonials, before/after, "cure"
- FACT-S/REPORTED (US): FTC **Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022)** applies to any health-related product (supplements, foods, OTC, devices, apps). Health claims generally need competent and reliable scientific evidence, typically RCTs. **Testimonials and expert endorsements are not a workaround.** There is no exception for homeopathic products. Sources: Cooley 2023-03-02 https://cooley.com/news/insight/2023/2023-03-02-ftc-revises-health-products-compliance-guidance ; BBB National Programs https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/ftc-health-claims-guidance
- REPORTED (US): The revised FTC **Endorsement Guides** took effect 26 Jul 2023. If the endorser's result is not typical, disclose what consumers generally achieve; "results not typical" alone is ineffective (DLA Piper, July 2023). The FTC **Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule** was announced 14 Aug 2024 and took effect 21 Oct 2024. It bans fake reviews and testimonials, **including AI-generated ones**, with civil penalties of about US$53,088 per violation (inflation-adjusted figure per FoodNavigator, 2026-01-07). Sources: Morgan Lewis https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/08/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials ; Hunton https://hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/ftc-issues-final-rule-targeting-fake-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials-including-those-generated-by-artificial-intelligence-ai-and-online-bots
- FACT-S (EU): **Reg. 1169/2011 art. 7(3)** says foods, including supplements, may not be attributed the property of **preventing, treating or curing** disease. **Reg. 1924/2006**: only health claims authorised in the EU Register may be used. Sources: FSAI https://www.fsai.ie/... ; Danish Veterinary and Food Administration https://en.foedevarestyrelsen.dk/food/labelling-and-marketing-of-food/voluntary-food-information/nutrition-and-health-claims ; EUR-Lex https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2006/1924
- REPORTED (CI): The AIRP's 2026 declaration campaign covered cosmetics, **compléments alimentaires** and phytomedicines: 11,849 products and 1,140 establishments were declared, with a compliance phase before stronger controls (Koaci 2026-09-17; Fraternité Matin). Health claims for these products also need advertising approval (AIP, Sept 2026).
- **Agent rule (INTERPRETATION):** treat "cure", "guaranteed", "100% effective", "no side effects", "safe for everyone", miracle or natural-equals-safe claims, and disease-treatment claims for non-medicines as **blocked**. Treat patient testimonials and before/after visuals as **blocked by default** for medicines (EU art. 90 / Gintec; CI decree content unverified). Synthetic or AI "patients" are never allowed as testimonials (FTC 2024 rule; platform AI-disclosure rules).

### B3. Platform policies
- **YouTube Medical Misinformation** (FACT-S, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13813322 , n.d.). It removes content that contradicts local health authorities (LHAs) or WHO on prevention, transmission, treatment or diagnosis. This includes promoting harmful substances, and treatments not approved by LHAs as safe and effective, with specific rules on cancer. It covers videos, descriptions, comments and live streams. Exceptions apply for educational, documentary, scientific or artistic context. Policy is updated as health-authority guidance changes.
- **Google Ads Healthcare and medicines** (FACT-S, https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13788386 , https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14776727 May 2024 update). Online pharmacies and telemedicine promoting Rx drugs need **LegitScript, NABP VIPPS or .pharmacy accreditation plus Google certification**. Rx-drug promotion in ads, landing pages and keywords is allowed only in listed countries by certified advertisers, and Rx opioid painkillers are never allowed. The country lists differ between page versions (UNVERIFIED). **Côte d'Ivoire did not appear in either version seen.** Expect Rx promotion to be disallowed for CI targeting. OTC rules vary by country and need a live check.
- **Meta** (FACT-S, https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals/ and https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/content-specific-restrictions/online-pharmacies , n.d.). Online pharmacies need **LegitScript certification plus Meta's prior written permission**, and must target **18+** only. LegitScript-certified advertisers may promote Rx drugs **only to the US, Canada or New Zealand**. Manufacturers have a Meta internal-review route (UK page). OTC-specific rule not found (UNVERIFIED). Meta's health-data rules also restrict custom audiences and pixel use on health sites (not verified here).
- **TikTok** (FACT-S, https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-healthcare-pharmaceuticals , updated 2025/2026). Healthcare is highly regulated and must follow the target country's local law. Pharma ads must target **18+**. Rx medicine ads are banned outright in some markets; OTC is allowed only with local approval or certification; pharmacies need LegitScript or equivalent in North America. REPORTED: healthcare is a restricted category needing pre-authorisation, and guaranteed health outcomes are prohibited in branded content (ConductAtlas). Check the **METAP (Middle East, Turkey, Africa, Pakistan) Ad Policy Handbook** for Côte d'Ivoire: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/ad-policy-handbook-metap?lang=en (not read).

### B4. AI disclosure
- **YouTube** (FACT-S partial, "Disclosing use of GenAI content" https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491 ; REPORTED: CNN 2024-03-18, YouTube blog https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content). Creators must tick "altered or synthetic content" in the upload flow when **realistic** content could be mistaken for a real person, place or event. Examples: making a **real person** appear to say or do something they didn't; **"synthetically generating a person's voice to narrate a video"** when using a realistic person's likeness; altering footage of real events; generating realistic scenes that never happened. **No disclosure** is needed for clearly unrealistic or animated content, special effects, or AI used only for production help (scripts, captions). YouTube may add the label itself; repeated non-disclosure risks removal or YPP penalties. **Health is a sensitive topic and gets a more prominent in-player label.** Disclosure does not reduce reach or monetisation (YouTube statement). REPORTED, single source: a May 2026 update adds Shorts overlays.
- **AI voiceover of a non-real voice over motion graphics (INTERPRETATION):** a generic stock or designed synthetic narrator over clearly animated graphics falls in the "animation / production assistance" zone and probably does not require ticking the box. Sources conflict on voiceover specifically. A **cloned voice of a real person** (e.g. the client's pharmacist or a doctor) requires disclosure, and so do realistic AI humans, patients or doctors. **For health content, default to ticking the box whenever a realistic human voice or likeness is synthetic.** It costs nothing and avoids an imposed label. Also confirm consent and rights for any cloned voice.
- **Meta** (REPORTED; primary help page not read). An "AI info" label is applied automatically when C2PA/IPTC metadata or Meta's own gen-AI ad tools are detected. **Mandatory disclosure in social issue, election and political ads** applies to photorealistic image, video or realistic audio digitally created or altered to show a real person saying or doing something they didn't (Yahoo/ThePrint, Canada election coverage). Claims that disclosure is now mandatory for all ads, and the related rejection-rate statistics, are UNVERIFIED (vendor blogs).
- **TikTok** (REPORTED: TechCrunch 2023-09-19; Music Business Worldwide; Digital Rights Monitor 2026-04-30). Creators must label AI-generated content containing **realistic images, audio or video**. TikTok auto-labels detected or C2PA content, and an auto-label cannot be removed.

### B5. Remotion licence (agency / client work)
- FACT-D: The pinned `studio/node_modules/remotion/LICENSE.md` (Remotion 4.0.541; same text on GitHub main) says the Free License covers **an individual; a for-profit organisation with up to 3 employees; a non-profit; or evaluation before commercial use**. Everyone else needs a **Company License** (remotion.pro/license). It also notes "In Remotion 5.0, the license will slightly change" (PR #3750). Commercial video creation is allowed under Free. Selling or relicensing a derivative of Remotion is not.
- FACT-D (Remotion docs source on GitHub main, `packages/docs/docs/license/faq.mdx`; also https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq):
  - **Agency delivering only finished files** (MP4, where the client never receives the code or runs Remotion): only the **agency's** headcount counts. 3 or fewer people means Free; 4 or more means a Company License. The client needs no licence.
  - **Collaboration** (client owns or operates the Remotion project): **both companies' headcounts are added** toward the 4-person threshold. The **entity that owns the IP of the Remotion project** buys the licence; an agency can manage it for the client.
  - **Freelancers, other studios and consultants** operating Remotion on the project **count toward the total**.
- REPORTED: Company License from about **US$25/seat/month**, or a render-based option at about US$0.01/render with a US$100/month minimum (Layer3 Labs, updated 2026-10-11; Tooliverse; Wireflow). Official prices were not read (UNVERIFIED).
- **Implication:** if the portable skill hands the Remotion project or code to a client, or a client team edits it, that is a collaboration and headcounts combine. Deliver finished renders by default, and record team size in the licence ledger.

## D. Pre-production compliance checklist (apply to every regulated-client script)

**Gate 0 — Classification (block until answered)**
- [ ] Product type: Rx-only medicine / OTC medicine / medical device / supplement / cosmetic / phytomedicine / pharmacy service / corporate brand only?
- [ ] Advertiser type: manufacturer / distributor / retail pharmacy (officine) / telehealth?
- [ ] Target markets and platforms (CI only? international? which countries?). Apply the strictest applicable rule set.
- [ ] Marketing authorisation (AMM) or registration number on file for each product named (CI: needed with the AIRP advertising file).

**Gate 1 — Can this be advertised at all?**
- [ ] **Rx-only product + public audience = STOP.** Offer instead: a disease-awareness or brand/corporate video with no product name, or HCP-only material through professional channels (UEMOA Décision 10/2010 guidelines apply).
- [ ] No call-to-action to buy medicines online or through social media DMs for a CI audience (AIRP: online sale not authorised).
- [ ] Paid ads: platform eligibility confirmed (Google certification + LegitScript; Meta written permission + LegitScript + 18+; TikTok local-law compliance + 18+). Rx ads outside allowed countries = STOP.

**Gate 2 — Claims and content (line by line)**
- [ ] Every efficacy or benefit claim is mapped to a source in the approved labelling (SmPC/notice/AMM) or to substantiation on file. Record it in the claim ledger.
- [ ] No "cure / guaranteed / miracle / 100% / no side effects / safe for everyone / natural = safe". No disease-treatment claims for supplements or cosmetics.
- [ ] No patient testimonials, before/after visuals, celebrity or health-professional endorsement of medicines, or fear-based messaging, unless the regulator expressly approves them. **Never synthetic or AI "patients", "doctors" or "reviews".**
- [ ] Nothing contradicts WHO or local health-authority guidance (YouTube medical-misinformation policy).
- [ ] Includes the mandatory information (product name, INN, indication, "read the notice", "ask your pharmacist or doctor", risks). CI mandatory mentions are unverified; get the list from the AIRP or the client's regulatory lead.
- [ ] Not aimed at minors; platform targeting set to 18+.

**Gate 3 — Fair balance (motion-design specific)**
- [ ] Risk and safety information is given **comparable prominence** to benefits: same voice, pace and volume; on-screen text legible for long enough; no competing animation, music swells or fast cuts during risk statements (FDA CCN standards; OPDP 2026 net-impression letters).

**Gate 4 — AI and media rights**
- [ ] Voice: commercial-licensed plan active at generation time (ElevenLabs Starter+). Library-voice terms checked. Cloned voices only with written consent of the voice owner.
- [ ] Disclosure: tick YouTube "altered or synthetic" and the TikTok AI label if any **realistic** human voice, face or scene is synthetic. Do not strip C2PA metadata to avoid Meta labels.
- [ ] All fonts, music, stock and images are licensed and logged . Remotion licence status recorded (headcount, delivery mode).

**Gate 5 — Approval and record**
- [ ] Client's responsible pharmacist or regulatory lead has signed off on script and storyboard.
- [ ] **CI: AIRP advertising authorisation obtained before any public broadcast or posting**; reference number logged.
- [ ] Owner approval recorded before any credit spend (TTS) and before any publication (a human publishes manually).
- [ ] Final script, sources, approvals and authorisation number saved in the project records.
