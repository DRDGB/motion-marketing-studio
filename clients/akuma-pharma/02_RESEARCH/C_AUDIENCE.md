## 0. Summary (what matters for B2B motion content)
1. **The real buyer is a licensed importer/distributor, and the product they need is registrability.** In every
   market they need a foreign maker whose documents get through registration: CoPP, GMP evidence, CTD/ACTD dossier,
   Zone IVb stability, and labels that meet local rules. The registration path is set per country (NAFDAC, PPB,
   FDA Ghana and others), not across Africa (C-024, C-035, C-036, C-055).
2. **"Made in India" helps and hurts.** India is the volume supplier: USD 3.93bn of pharma exports to Africa in
   FY25 (C-001, C-002). But since 2022 a series of contaminated-syrup alerts (C-007–C-010), NAFDAC blacklistings
   (C-028), the Kenya PPB stance on the Indian Pharmacopoeia (C-032) and recalls (C-033) mean buyers and
   regulators now check Indian suppliers harder. Content has to *show proof*, not claim quality (C-057).
3. **2025–2026 tightened the market.** Nigeria is substituting imports with local production (5+5 / Ceiling
   List: imports of listed products fell 70%; import:local ratio went from 70:30 to 50:50, C-027) and checks
   shipments from India before they leave (pre-shipment inspection, C-030). Kenya stopped parallel imports and is
   rolling out track-and-trace (C-034). India's MSME GMP deadline passed on 31 Dec 2025 (C-011, C-012). The
   African Medicines Agency is now operating but does not replace national registration (C-019, C-020).
4. **Channels: WhatsApp and LinkedIn first, YouTube for reference, trade events for trust.** Buyers use WhatsApp
   everywhere (C-047). LinkedIn reach is large in South Africa and Nigeria, smaller in Francophone markets (C-042–C-046).
5. **Peptides split in two.** Peptide APIs (semaglutide/tirzepatide) are a real 2026 opportunity in markets where
   the patent has lapsed (C-039, C-040). RUO "research peptides" draw mostly consumer and grey-market search
   interest and carry regulatory risk. Keep them out of social content until a reviewer clears them (C-041, C-056).

### Segment A: Licensed importers / distributors / wholesalers
**Who decides (roles, not people):** owner/MD (commercial bet, exclusivity, credit); **superintendent or
regulatory pharmacist** (legally responsible in Nigeria for wholesale/import premises, C-026); regulatory affairs
officer (dossier, registration); procurement/supply chain manager (lead time, MOQ, stock); finance (FX, LC terms).

**Jobs-to-be-done**
- "Find a manufacturer whose dossier will pass *my* regulator the first time, so I can register products I'm
  allowed to import and sell."
- "Keep fast-moving lines (antibiotics, antimalarials, anti-diabetics) in stock without quality incidents."
- "Win or keep exclusivity / local-agent rights for registered products (the asset that holds value)."
- "Protect my licence and reputation: no recalls, no blacklisting, no counterfeit look-alikes."

**Pains**

|---|---|---|
| Slow, unpredictable registration (NAFDAC timelines quoted from 120 working days to 6–7 months) | Per country. Joint assessments (ZaZiBoNa, EAC, WA-MRH) also slow at the national step | C-025, C-022 |

**Objections to Indian suppliers (what they think but may not say)**
- "Is your WHO-GMP real, and current?" The search query *who gmp certificate verification* exists (C-048). Many
  buyers do not know that an Indian WHO-GMP is a national certificate under the WHO Certification Scheme, **not**
  WHO prequalification (C-013, C-014).
- "Remember the cough syrups." The Gambia 2022 (66 child deaths reported, C-007, C-008), Uzbekistan/Indonesia
  alerts (C-009) and the Coldrif case in India in Oct 2025 (C-010) shape perceptions of Indian *oral liquids*.
  This matters because Akuma sells oral liquids.
- "Small Indian plants may not meet the new Schedule M." Only ~26% of MSMEs were expected to comply by the
  deadline (C-012).
- "My regulator rejected Indian-standard products before" (Kenya IP notice, C-032). "NAFDAC blacklisted an Indian
  firm" (C-028).
- "Will you register in my name and stick with me, or sell to my competitor next door?" (INFERENCE from the
  local-agent model, C-024)

**Triggers**
- A registration window opens, or a competitor's product is recalled or withdrawn (C-029, C-033).
- A product moves on or off a localisation list (Nigeria, C-027).
- Patent expiries (semaglutide, 2026) and new-generic launches (C-039).
- Trade delegations and fairs: **Pharmexcil buyer-seller meets in Nairobi (30 Nov–1 Dec), Accra (3–4 Dec) and
  Luanda (7–8 Dec 2026)** (C-049). **CPHI Middle East, Riyadh, 14–16 Dec 2026** (C-050).
- FX relief, or a funding gap at a donor channel that pushes procurement to private importers (C-018, INFERENCE).

**Proof they require before a first order** (OPINION built on the regulatory requirements, C-057)
CoPP (WHO format) per product and country · GMP certificate plus the latest full inspection report (Ghana waiver
logic, C-035) · CTD/ACTD dossier modules (sample index) · **Zone IVb (30 °C/75 % RH) long-term stability** (C-036) ·
batch CoA and the pharmacopoeia used (BP/USP/Ph.Eur. vs IP, C-032) · existing registrations in other African
markets · samples · a plant video or virtual tour · named QC lab capability · clear exclusivity and agency terms.

**Per-country notes (do not generalise)**

- **SADC (incl. South Africa via SAHPRA, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Namibia):** ZaZiBoNa collaborative route
  (C-021, C-023). SAHPRA is the most demanding market (INFERENCE). People also search *sahpra section 21*

- **Francophone West/Central Africa (Côte d'Ivoire AIRP, Senegal, Cameroon, Mali, Togo…):** French-language

### Segment B: Institutional / tender buyers
**Who:** national central medical stores and procurement agencies, public and mission hospitals, NGOs, and
programmes funded by the Global Fund, UNICEF and (formerly) USAID/PMI. **Decision makers:** procurement
committees, QA/pharmacy officers, programme managers. Donor programmes run their own QA and prequalification.

**What they require (verified)**
- Global Fund: every product must comply with national registration in the country of use. **ARV, TB and malaria
  products must be WHO PQ, SRA, WLA or ERP-recommended**. ERP approval is time-limited (≤12 months) (C-015, C-016).
  → Akuma's artesunate injection would need WHO PQ, SRA approval or an ERP route for Global Fund-funded malaria
  procurement (INFERENCE).
- UNICEF: an abridged route exists only for products already WHO PQ, SRA or WLA approved. Risk-based GMP
  inspections (C-017).
- Random QC testing of products bought with Global Fund money, by ISO 17025 or WHO-prequalified labs (REPORTED in
  a search excerpt of Global Fund docs; re-verify).

**Pains:** donor funding shocks and supply gaps in 2025–26 (C-018). Falsified antimalarials and antibiotics in
circulation (C-005, C-006). Cost pressure.
**Objections:** "Not WHO-prequalified = not eligible." "No track record in tenders."
**Triggers:** national (domestic-funded) tenders when donor supply drops (INFERENCE). Emergency procurement.
**Content angle:** explain the quality system and the registrations in hand. Do **not** imply eligibility for
donor tenders without WHO PQ or SRA approval (compliance risk).

### Segment C: Local manufacturers / formulators buying APIs
**Who:** African formulators expanding under localisation (Nigeria now has 190 local manufacturers, C-027),
contract manufacturers, and South African/Egyptian/Kenyan firms looking at GLP-1 generics. **Decision makers:**
technical director, QA/QC head, regulatory affairs (DMF/ASMF), procurement.

**JTBD:** "Secure a qualified, consistent API source with the documents my regulator accepts, at a price that lets
me compete with finished imports."
**Pains:** Africa's API import dependence of over 95% (C-004). FX. Supplier qualification audits. For peptides:
synthesis complexity, impurity profiles and cold chain (REPORTED: peptide synthesis is a different discipline from
small-molecule APIs. Seen in a search summary, not ledgered).
**Objections:** "Can an exporter (not a known API maker) guarantee peptide API quality and continuity?" "Is the
molecule off-patent in *my* country?" (patent status varies. US/EU protection is reported to run to 2031+, C-039 note).
**Triggers:** semaglutide patent expiry in India (Mar 2026) and the opening in Kenya and other markets (C-039,
C-040). Localisation incentives (C-027).
**Proof required:** DMF/ASMF or open-part dossier, GMP certificate of the **API site**, CoA and impurity profile,
stability, audit access, supply-continuity commitment. **Open question:** does Akuma make these APIs or source
them? This decides what can be claimed.

### Segment D: Research labs buying RUO peptides (correction)
- No African institutional demand signal was found. Autocomplete for *research peptides* returns *for sale*, *for
  weight loss*, *reddit*, *reviews* (C-048, C-056). These are consumer and grey-market intents.
- Regulators see this space as a risk: in the US, BPC-157 is a safety-flagged bulk substance with an unresolved
  compounding status (C-041). Retatrutide is an investigational molecule (approval status not verified at 2026-11-10).
- **Recommendation (OPINION):** no RUO peptides in social or motion content. If the client insists, use a gated B2B
  page with "not for human use" wording reviewed by a regulatory professional. Flag to compliance (Track E).

## 5. Market context (dated)
- **India → Africa:** USD 3,933.65m in FY2024-25 (12.91% of India's pharma exports, −0.42% y/y). Top markets:
  South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana (Pharmexcil, 2025-06-14. C-001, C-002). **India's share of Africa's medicine
  imports is not verified.** A "~40%" figure circulates without a traceable calculation. Use UN Comtrade (HS 30)
  before quoting any share.
- **Import dependence:** Africa imports over 70% of its medicines (AUDA-NEPAD via press, Jan 2025, C-003) and over
  95% of its APIs (REPORTED, C-004).
- **Substandard/falsified medicines:** 1 in 10 in LMICs (WHO, 2017-11-28, C-005). An estimated 116k extra malaria
  deaths a year in sub-Saharan Africa from substandard and falsified antimalarials (modelled, C-006).
- **AMA:** full operations in Kigali since Oct 2025 (C-019). 31 ratifications by Jan 2026 (C-020). National
  registration still governs market entry (INFERENCE).
- **Regional harmonisation:** ZaZiBoNa has held 44 sessions and assessed 350+ products (SADC, 2024-02-22, C-021).
  Joint procedures are criticised for slow national follow-through (C-022). A Botswana ZaZiBoNa guideline has been
  in effect since 2025-07-02 (C-023). ECOWAS WA-MRH and EAC-MRH exist (C-022). No 2025–26 statistics were found.
- **Enforcement 2025–2026:** NAFDAC blacklisted an Indian firm (Feb 2025, C-028), withdrew 101 products (Sep 2025,
  C-029), runs pre-shipment inspection of imports from India and China (C-030) and destroyed ₦939m of goods (Mar
  2026, C-031). Kenya PPB issued the IP notice (Jan 2025, C-032), recalled Indian-made batches (Apr 2025, C-033),
  stopped parallel imports and is rolling out track-and-trace (Jul–Aug 2026, C-034).
- **Indian supply side:** revised Schedule M deadline for MSMEs was 31 Dec 2025 with low expected compliance
  (C-011, C-012). WHO-GMP/COPP applications have been online-only via ONDLS since 15 Aug 2025 (C-013).
- **Donor channel shock:** USAID dissolved, PMI capacity affected, GHSC-PSM being ended (Apr 2026, C-018).
