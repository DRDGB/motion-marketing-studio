## 1. Summary

1. **The market is large but flat.** India shipped USD 3.93 bn of pharma to Africa in FY2024-25, 12.91% of
   its USD 30.47 bn pharma exports, down 0.42% on the year (D-001, D-002). Formulations make up about
   75% of the export basket (D-004). Within Africa, Nigeria (+5.4%) and Ghana (+22.6%) grew while South
   Africa fell 5.6% (D-003). Nigeria was the fastest-growing major destination in Apr–Nov FY26 (D-006).
2. **Buyers face more demanding regulators and policies that favour local factories.** Ten African
   regulators now operate at WHO Maturity Level 3, among them NAFDAC, Ghana FDA, TMDA, EFDA and Rwanda
   FDA (D-012). The African Medicines Agency has been operational in Kigali since October 2025 (D-011).
   Nigeria, Kenya and Ethiopia are all pushing local manufacturing, with duty waivers, a target of 50%
   local supply and tender price preferences (D-013 to D-017).
3. **Trust is the big unaddressed theme.** The videos about "Indian pharma and Africa" with the most
   views come from the BBC: the opioid investigation has 545,943 views on BBC News Africa and 123,623 on
   BBC News India (D-033). WHO alerts about Indian-made contaminated syrups in The Gambia (2022) and
   Cameroon (2023) are on the record (D-027, D-031). India responded with mandatory testing of cough
   syrups in government labs before export, a revised Schedule M, and a tighter export NOC checklist
   (D-030, D-032, D-035). **No comparable exporter explains, visually, the controls it applies.**
4. **Small exporters post almost no video.** Their YouTube channels are dormant or off-topic, with
   double- or triple-digit views. They are English-only and rely on WhatsApp buttons and SEO landing pages
   per country (D-040 to D-044). Export-training creators and the BBC hold the attention instead (D-049).
5. **Peptides are both an opportunity and a risk.** Semaglutide went generic in India on 20–21 March
   2026, and Indian brands are entering Africa: Sun Pharma received South African approval and Hetero is
   selling in Africa (D-019, D-020). Chinese API makers such as Sinopep and Hybio are turning to
   supplying generic makers (D-022). US regulators are actively pursuing sellers of "research use only"
   (RUO) peptides (D-023 to D-025). **RUO products should stay out of public content.**

## 3. Trends that shape content

- **Regional regulation is maturing.** The AMA became operational in October 2025 with headquarters in
  Kigali (D-011). WHO lists these African regulators at ML3: Egypt, Ethiopia (2025), Ghana, Mozambique
  (2026), Nigeria, Rwanda, Senegal, South Africa (vaccines), Tanzania and Zimbabwe (D-012). The excerpt
  reviewed does not show Kenya's PPB, so confirm before stating PPB's status.
  → Content angle: "dossiers built for ML3 reviewers", not "fast registration".
- **Policies favour local manufacturing.**
  - Nigeria launched PVAC in October 2023 and an Executive Order removed duties and VAT on pharma inputs
    (D-013). An IFC interview quotes "we import 70 percent" (D-014).
  - Kenya's strategy targets 50% local supply of essential health products by 2026. It cites EAC measures
    including a 25% tariff where local capacity is sufficient and a 20% price preference in public
    tenders (D-015). The strategy was launched on 23 June 2026 (D-016).
  - Ethiopia is presented as an emerging manufacturing hub, with the Kilinto park and an ML3 regulator
    (D-017).
  - MMV wants to grow the number of WHO-prequalified African antimalarial manufacturers it supports from
    3 to 7 by 2030 (D-018).
  - → INFERENCE (D-053): pure import-substitution messaging will age badly. The client gains more by
    showing partnership: semi-finished goods, technology and dossier support, API supply to local plants,
    and products that are not made locally, such as sterile injectables and peptides.
- **GLP-1 peptides are going generic.**
  - India's semaglutide patent lapsed on 20 March 2026. At least six companies launched on 21 March, at
    INR 1,300–4,200 a month against INR 8,800+ for the originator (D-019).
  - Pharmarack counted 26 brands from 13 companies in March. One major launcher paused supply in July
    after an API batch failed specification. Sun Pharma was approved in South Africa in July, Hetero is
    selling in Africa, and China's patent also expired on 20 March 2026, with no NMPA approvals by the
    cut-off date (D-020). Dr Reddy's launched in Canada on 15 May 2026 (D-021).
  - → INFERENCE: generic makers buying API care about the quality of the API source. A supplier that
    shows its batch-release and impurity controls is answering a question buyers have right now.
- **Scrutiny of RUO peptides is rising.**
  - FDA warning letters say "Research Use Only" labels do not protect a seller whose website implies
    human use (D-023). Five more sellers received letters in August 2026, and the owner of a US vendor
    was sentenced to 70 months in July 2026 (D-024).
  - In July 2026 an FDA advisory panel recommended BPC-157, TB-500 and others for the compounding list.
    The recommendation is non-binding and rulemaking is pending (D-025).
  - → OPINION (D-052): keep the client's RUO line (BPC-157, TB-500, Retatrutide) **out of all social
    content**. Route this to Track E and compliance.

### What the category posts and which hooks work
- **Small Indian exporters:** logo stings, catalogue carousels, flag grids, "exporter to [country]"
  pages, and WhatsApp CTAs. Videos are almost non-existent and draw tiny audiences (13–485 views). Seen
  content is English-only (INFERENCE from titles and pages, D-050).
- **Chinese peptide API makers:** facility films and trade-fair booth clips. The best result is a GMP
  facility film at about 1.9K views. Hooks are "GMP", "scale", "end-to-end CMC".
- **What earns attention on this topic (YouTube search, seen 2026-11-10):**
  - "How to export medicines from India" explainers by trainers: 59,261 / 50,896 / 34,437 views.
  - Investigations ("India's Opioid Kings": 545,943).
  - Short "What are peptides?" explainers (Bachem: 15,939).
  - Purpose manifestos (Cipla Africa: 268K–499K).
  - Hook patterns that work: a **plain question** ("What are peptides?", "How to export…"), **inside
    view** ("Inside…", facility tour), and **stakes and trust** ("Who is flooding…").

### Trade dress to avoid copying
- **Cipla Africa:** "Caring for life" line and manifesto style (warm human close-ups, handwritten
  type).
- **Bachem:** emoji-question titles ("What are peptides? 🤔") and its blue molecular look. Borrow the
  *format* (60–90 s question-led explainer), not the look.
- **Taj Pharma:** emoji-heavy titles, "Health Comes Before Profit" phrasing.
- **Peers in general:** flag-grid "we export to" slides and the generic "WHO-GMP" badge as a hero.
  These are common to every peer and make the client look the same as them.
- **Avoid red-on-white "alert" graphics** that look like WHO or regulator alerts. The client's crimson
  (#B3122B) must not resemble a warning notice when used in trust content.

### Example videos (animated motion design, no people on camera)

1. **"Before it ships: 7 checks on every batch" (45–60 s, 9:16 and 1:1).** A crimson line follows a vial
   from API receipt through excipient testing, in-process QC, stability chamber, CoA, export NOC and
   destination-registration stamp, ending at a map pin. One check per beat, each with an icon and a 4–6
   word label. Close on "Ask for the CoA on WhatsApp." Steps must match the client's real SOPs (open
   question). Do not mention DEG cases or other companies by name. Frame it positively: "here is what we
   verify".
2. **"Why 25 °C stability data isn't enough for Lagos" (30–45 s).** A split screen of thermometer and
   humidity gauges climbs from Zone II to Zone IVb (30 °C / 75% RH), and a tablet or vial icon stays
   stable as the chart extends to the shelf-life month. End card: "Zone IVb stability data available per
   product." Show only products for which the client actually holds IVb data.
3. **"Registering in Ghana: the 5-step map" (60–90 s, series).** An animated route map of dossier
   module icons (CTD 1–5) moving to an FDA Ghana milestone timeline. Then repeat for NAFDAC, TMDA, EFDA,
   PPB and SAHPRA. Use public regulator facts only. Timelines and fees come from the client or the
   regulator's site, re-checked at production (Track E).
4. **"Same video, your language" (FR/PT/AR versions of #1 and #2).** Kinetic typography in each language
   with RTL layout for Arabic, voiced or captioned. Pin the regional WhatsApp desk number. A native
   reviewer must sign off each language.
5. **"What's in a peptide API dossier?" (60–90 s, LinkedIn / YouTube).** An exploded-view animation of a
   peptide chain with impurity-profile bars, a related-substances table, a stability panel and a DMF/CoA
   stack. The audience is licensed formulators only: no dosing, no weight-loss claims, no RUO products.
   Hold it until compliance review (Track E).
