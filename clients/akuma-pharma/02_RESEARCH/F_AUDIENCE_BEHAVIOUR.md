# Track F: How the African audience watches, shares and reacts to video and ads
_2026-11-11 · added after the CEO's feedback on AKV-001 v1 · claims in RESEARCH_LEDGER.csv (F-001…F-006)_

## What the evidence says
1. **Ads that get talked about** have cultural relevance, creative bravery/surprise, a positive emotional response, and
   speak to an issue the audience cares about: "an experience worthy of discussion that the brand plays a part in, rather
   than an ad that simply sells your product" (Ipsos Kenya, 2022) [F-001, F-002].
2. **Reality wins.** In a six-country survey (GH, KE, NG, ZA, TZ, UG), lifestyle images and videos of real people were the most
   popular ad content; 95% said online ads influence their purchases at least to some extent; Facebook was the favourite
   channel (45%) (GeoPoll, survey Dec 2020, published Jan 2021) [F-003, F-004, F-005].
3. **Storyline first.** Nigerian creators: "no matter who you are or the kind of video you're making, you must have a storyline"
   (MDPI 2025 study of Nigerian TikTok influencers) [F-006].
4. From Track C (unchanged): the buyer's real pains are stock-outs, suppliers that delay, document burden, distance and
   trust; WhatsApp is where business conversations happen [C_AUDIENCE §1–3].

## What it means for every Akuma Pharma video (creative rules, D-022)
- **A scenario, not a lecture.** Each video is a short story the viewer lives every week: a real country on the map of Africa,
  its flag, a city, a pharmacy or distributor, the owner and staff, the shelves, a problem (empty shelf, "next month",
  missing document), then Akuma Pharma as the option they had not considered, then the action.
- **The viewer must recognise himself in the first 3 seconds**: place (map zoom + flag), person (owner + staff), shelves.
- **Direct, conversational English** the way business people in Lagos, Accra or Nairobi talk: short sentences, quoted
  dialogue ("Oga, next month."), speaking to the owner by name, no corporate abstractions.
- **Positive ending**: the problem is solved on screen (shelves restocked, owner relieved), then the CTA.
- **Characters are drawn and labelled "Illustrative story"**; they are never presented as real customers or testimonials.
- Rotate countries across the series (Nigeria → Ghana → Côte d'Ivoire (FR) → Kenya …) so each market sees itself.
