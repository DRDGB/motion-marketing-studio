## 1. Context in one paragraph
CI's *strict* unemployment rate is very low (2.3% in 2022) and is publicly disputed. About 9 jobs in 10
are informal (89–94%, 2016–2019) (C-001). In 2014 the rate rose from 5.3% to 26.5% once underemployment
was counted (C-003). The problem is less "no work at all" than "petits boulots, no answers, no real
job". Senegal's broad unemployment rate is 22.9% in Q1 2026, with youth at 28.4% (C-004), and about one
young person in three is NEET (C-006). In Cameroon, ~87% of jobs are informal and labour
underutilisation is 26.3% (C-007, C-008). In a 38-country Afrobarometer survey, young people name
*lack of adequate training* (26–27%) and *lack of experience* (19–20%) as the top obstacles (C-011).
Those two pains map directly to "CV sans expérience". In CI, only 6% say a young person in their
household benefited from a public youth programme (C-010).

## 2. Segments

### S1. "Primo-demandeurs": students, new graduates and BTS/Licence/Master holders looking for a *stage* or first job (CORE, ~60% of effort)
- **Jobs-to-be-done:** "Make me a credible CV even though I have nothing to put on it." Write a
  *lettre de motivation stage*, find a real *stage* or first job, and get at least one reply.
- **Pains:** no CV template and no idea of the format ("CV ivoirien", Word or Canva?) (C-032, C-034). Nothing
  to fill the "expérience" box (C-033, C-011). Sending dozens of CVs with no answer. Not knowing what an
  ATS is (C-036). Scams on Facebook and WhatsApp (C-025). Paying a cyber café to type or print the CV
  (anecdotal; no priced source found, so ask the client). Lack of network (*le piston*).
- **Triggers:** BAC/BTS results (early July) (C-044–C-046). End of semester 2 and the *stage de fin de
  cycle* (June) (C-047). University *rentrée* (mid-Sept) (C-047). Job fairs (FNER in May, FIMEC on
  15 July) (C-049, C-050). Agence Emploi Jeunes campaigns (C-041).
- **Objections:** "Je peux le faire sur Word/Canva" (C-043). "CV gratuit … sans payer" (C-038). "L'IA
  va inventer des choses". "Encore une arnaque qui veut mon argent" (C-057).
- **Proof that moves them:** before/after CVs, an ATS score that goes up live, a real *stage* offer
  marked "vérifiée", and a first step that costs nothing.

### S2. Young professionals switching or climbing (25–35, salaried, often in Abidjan, Dakar or Douala)
- **Jobs-to-be-done:** leave a precarious or informal job for a better one, and look modern on LinkedIn
  (1.80M LinkedIn members in CI, 1.50M in SN, 1.60M in CM) (C-015–C-017).
- **Pains:** an old Word CV that no longer fits the role, rejection by ATS keyword filters, no time to
  rewrite the CV for each offer, wanting to stand out (portfolio or personal site).
- **Triggers:** January (new year, new budgets), appraisal season, a layoff, an offer seen on LinkedIn
  or Educarriere (C-024).
- **Objections:** "Mon CV est déjà bon", worry about data privacy (current employer seeing it), price.
- **Product hooks:** ATS score, AI cover letter per offer, CV turned into a portfolio website, match
  score.

### S3. Low-qualified and informal-sector job seekers (BEPC/BAC level: drivers, BTP, sales, call centres, domestic work)
- **Evidence:** searches for "offre d'emploi abidjan niveau bac / niveau bepc / chauffeur / BTP",
  "cv simple", "comment faire un cv sur son téléphone", "cv word à remplir" and "comment demander un
  emploi par message" (C-032, C-037, C-042).
- **Jobs-to-be-done:** a clean one-page CV made on the phone in 5 minutes and sent on WhatsApp.
- **Pains:** low literacy in formal French, no computer, high data cost (C-021), and the highest
  exposure to scams that ask for "frais de dossier" (C-025–C-028).
- **Objections:** price (each FCFA counts), "c'est pour les diplômés".
- **Fit:** a mobile-first, simple template, paid with Mobile Money. Videos should be voice-led and
  very visual.

### S4. Experienced professionals, managers and international or diaspora applicants
- **Evidence:** "cv canadien gratuit / 2026 / en anglais", "comment trouver un emploi au Canada / en
  Belgique / au Luxembourg depuis le Cameroun", "cv en anglais", "educarriere emploi ONG" (C-040).
- **Jobs-to-be-done:** an international-format CV (Canadian, European, NGO or UN), a bilingual CV, and
  a personal website as a calling card.
- **Pains:** different format rules per country, English-language CVs, credibility with foreign
  recruiters.
- **Objections:** "outil local = pas au niveau international", privacy, preference for a human coach.

### S5. (Later, for ChapChapTalents) Recruiters and SMEs
Not a priority for B2C content now. Pain: being flooded by unsuitable CVs with no tool to screen them.
Scams that impersonate companies hurt their brand (C-026, C-028). Keep for a later phase.

**Devices, data and payments**
- Phone-first. CI has 49.7M cellular connections for 13.4M internet users (C-014) and 35.5M mobile
  internet subscriptions (C-020).
- Data is costly: about USD 1.18/GB in 2023 (C-021). → Keep videos short and light, captions
  burned in, readable without sound (C-058).
- Mobile Money is mainstream. CI has 27.7M accounts (Orange 16.5M, MTN 8.2M, Moov 3.1M; Wave is outside
  the ARTCI split) (C-020). SN mobile money account ownership is ~67% of adults (C-022). "arnaque wave"
  appears in autocomplete (C-023). → Show credits paid in FCFA by Orange Money, MTN MoMo or Wave
  as a *normal* step, with a clear receipt and refund rule. **Never** let it look like "frais de
  dossier" (C-057).

**Language and register (C-029–C-031)**
- **CI:** French is the base. Nouchi is used for warmth and humour, sparingly: a hook line or a
  reaction, never in the product's promises. Familiar-register examples such as *on dit quoi*, *c'est
  comment*, *y'a pas drap* are unsourced and must be validated. Native reviewers must validate every slang line, because Nouchi
  changes fast and misuse sounds fake.
- **SN:** urban Wolof-French code-switching is the norm on Facebook and WhatsApp. Use mostly French, with
  Wolof greetings or interjections only when a Senegalese reviewer approves.
- **CM:** Camfranglais is a youth identity marker online, so use it only in CM-targeted variants.
- **Tone:** humour and short sketches (recruiter vs candidate, "CV de tonton", "le cyber du quartier"),
  aspiration (first salary, the family's pride), and "le grand frère / la grande sœur qui t'explique".
  Avoid lecturing.

## 4. Top pains (ranked, INFERENCE from C-001–C-043)
1. **"Je n'ai pas d'expérience"**: nothing to put on the CV (C-011, C-033).
2. **"J'envoie, on ne répond pas"**: no feedback, the CV is not adapted to the offer, ATS filters
   (C-036).
3. **"Les offres sont fausses ou périmées"**: scams and stale posts (C-025–C-028, C-037).
4. **"Je ne sais pas quel modèle utiliser"**: Word vs Canva vs "CV ivoirien" vs "CV canadien"
   (C-032, C-034, C-040).
5. **"Lettre de motivation = corvée"**: they want a short, simple, ready-to-adapt letter (C-035).
6. **"Je n'ai pas le réseau / le piston"**: perceived unfairness; a theme only, with no stat.
7. **"Ça coûte"**: data, printing, typing services; strong "gratuit / sans payer" intent (C-038).

## 7. Implications for strategy (hand-off to synthesis)
1. **Lead with S1 on TikTok.** Use FR + light Nouchi, before/after CV transformations, and an ATS score
   reveal as the visual hook (C-018, C-019, C-055).
2. **Make trust the brand's spine.** Run an "Anti-arnaque" series ("Un vrai recruteur ne te demande
   jamais de frais de dossier") and show how offers are verified. This turns the biggest objection
   into differentiation (C-025–C-028, C-057).
3. **Answer the price objection head-on.** Make clear what is free, what costs FCFA credits, and pay
   by Mobile Money in two taps (C-038, C-020).
4. **Build country variants, not one pan-African voice.** CI first; SN and CM variants get local
   reviewers for slang (C-029–C-031).
5. **Keep it data-light.** Use 15–35 s vertical clips with captions and small files, and make every
   video WhatsApp-Status-friendly (C-021, C-058).
